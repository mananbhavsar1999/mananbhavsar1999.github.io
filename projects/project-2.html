Overview

The Challenge

WinGD marine engines require robust auxiliary piping systems — lube oil, fuel, cooling water, and drain — that perform reliably in demanding offshore and marine environments. The task was to design these systems from reference, study the existing architecture, identify reliability gaps, and deliver improved new systems with full 3D documentation.

Marine auxiliary systems carry strict requirements for layout compactness, material selection, and system redundancy. Every pipe run, fitting, and connection had to be precisely modeled and documented for downstream fabrication and installation.

Approach

What I Did

  • Studied WinGD reference system designs for lube oil, fuel, cooling water, and drain piping — analyzing existing architecture to identify reliability improvement opportunities.
  • Designed new auxiliary piping systems in Siemens NX, incorporating reliability-driven modifications while maintaining system function and integration with the engine package.
  • Built complete 3D piping models for lube oil supply/return circuits, fuel piping networks, cooling water systems, and drain arrangements.
  • Performed pump selection, pipe sizing, and pressure/flow calculations to validate system performance across operating conditions.
  • Prepared and managed full BOM and MTO packages in Teamcenter, ensuring procurement alignment with the 3D model.
  • Prepared manufacturing and assembly drawings for all piping sub-systems to support fabrication teams.
3D piping models, system schematics, and assembly drawings from this project are available for discussion during interviews. Contact me directly for a technical walkthrough.
Outcome

Results & Learnings

The redesigned auxiliary systems incorporated targeted reliability improvements over the reference design, delivering new fully documented systems ready for fabrication. All models and BOMs were released through Teamcenter with full revision control.

This project deepened practical knowledge of marine auxiliary system design — particularly the integration of lube oil and fuel systems with large marine engine packages, and the discipline required to balance reliability improvements against system complexity.
