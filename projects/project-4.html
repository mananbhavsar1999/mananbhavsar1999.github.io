Overview

The Challenge

A marine engine's lubrication system is mission-critical — it keeps every bearing, journal, and gear surface protected under extreme loads and variable operating conditions at sea. Failure is not an option. The challenge was to design a complete lube oil system for a large marine engine: from sump to bearing and back, including all pumps, heat exchangers, filters, and control devices.

Marine environments add additional complexity — the system had to account for vessel inclination, sea state motion, and the need for redundancy so that a single component failure never leads to engine shutdown.

Approach

What I Did

  • Calculated total lube oil flow requirements based on engine bearing loads, journal dimensions, clearances, and target oil film temperature.
  • Selected main and standby lube oil pumps — sizing for flow, pressure, and NPSH requirements with appropriate safety margins.
  • Designed the lube oil cooler (shell & tube heat exchanger) — performed thermal sizing calculation to determine surface area, number of tubes, and cooling water flow.
  • Sized duplex filters for particle retention and specified filter change-over logic for maintenance without shutdown.
  • Developed the complete system P&ID including temperature control valve, pressure control valve, low pressure alarms, and automatic standby pump start logic.
  • Performed pressure drop calculations across the entire system to verify adequate lube oil pressure at all bearing inlet points.
  • Coordinated with engine OEM to verify interface requirements and obtain approval on system design.

Outcome

Results & Learnings

The lube oil system design was reviewed and approved by the engine OEM with no major technical comments — a strong validation of the engineering methodology applied. The thermal sizing of the heat exchanger was particularly commended for its conservative approach, ensuring the system performs reliably even in tropical sea conditions.

This project deepened my expertise in fluid system design, thermal engineering, and the importance of redundancy in safety-critical marine applications. Working directly with an OEM also gave valuable insight into how suppliers evaluate and validate customer-designed auxiliary systems.
