Overview

The Challenge

Gearbox design sits at the intersection of precision and performance — a few microns of misalignment or an incorrect bearing selection can cascade into catastrophic failure in industrial rotating equipment. The challenge here was to design a complete gearbox layout from scratch: defining the gear arrangement, calculating gear ratios, selecting appropriate bearings, and translating it all into manufacturable 3D geometry and engineering drawings.

The design had to balance compactness, load capacity, thermal behaviour, and manufacturing feasibility — all while working within a PLM environment with strict revision control using Teamcenter.

Approach

What I Did

  • Designed complete gearbox layout — defined gear type, number of stages, gear ratios, and shaft arrangements based on input/output speed and torque requirements.
  • Selected bearings for each shaft based on radial and axial load calculations, speed, and expected service life per ISO 281 standards.
  • Developed detailed 3D models of the gearbox assembly including housing, shafts, gears, bearings, seals, and end covers using Siemens NX.
  • Created manufacturing drawings with GD&T callouts, tolerances, and surface finish requirements for all machined components.
  • Performed design modification cycles — incorporating review feedback and ensuring all changes were tracked and released through Teamcenter.
  • Coordinated with manufacturing team to validate machining feasibility and assembly sequence.

Outcome

Results & Learnings

The gearbox design successfully passed internal design review with commendation on the quality of drawings and the thoroughness of the bearing selection rationale. Design modifications were completed within schedule with zero revision errors in the Teamcenter release process.

This project sharpened my understanding of gear geometry, load path analysis, and the critical importance of GD&T in ensuring assembly accuracy for rotating components. It also reinforced best practices for PLM-based design workflows where traceability is as important as the design itself.
