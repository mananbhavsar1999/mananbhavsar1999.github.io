Overview

The Challenge

In steam turbine design, dimensional accuracy is not merely a quality concern — it directly determines whether a machine runs reliably or fails catastrophically. Blade tip clearances measured in tenths of a millimetre, bearing fits within micron ranges, and thermal expansion gaps that must be maintained under operating temperatures from ambient to over 500°C. The challenge was to perform rigorous tolerance stack-up analysis on critical rotating assemblies and validate structural integrity through FEA.

The analysis had to account for manufacturing variation across multiple components, assembly sequence effects, and temperature-induced dimensional changes — all within a strict PLM-controlled engineering environment at Siemens.

Approach

What I Did

  • Built complete tolerance stack-up models in 3DCS for steam turbine rotating assemblies — defining datum schemes, GD&T callouts, and assembly constraints.
  • Performed worst-case and statistical (RSS) stack-up analyses to determine the cumulative effect of manufacturing tolerances on critical assembly dimensions.
  • Identified tolerance contributors with the highest sensitivity and recommended tighter tolerances where required and relaxed tolerances where feasible to reduce manufacturing cost.
  • Set up ANSYS FEA models for key turbine components — defined material properties, boundary conditions, and loading scenarios including centrifugal loads, steam pressure, and thermal gradients.
  • Analysed stress distribution, deformation, and factor of safety for components under operating conditions and identified areas of concern for design modification.
  • Prepared analysis reports documenting methodology, assumptions, results, and design recommendations for design review.
  • Released all analysis documentation through Teamcenter with full revision control.

Outcome

Results & Learnings

The tolerance analysis identified three critical stack-up paths where worst-case manufacturing variation could lead to inadequate blade tip clearances — recommendations were incorporated into the drawing revisions. The FEA study confirmed structural adequacy of the analysed components with safety factors well within Siemens' internal design standards.

This project was a masterclass in the relationship between design intent, manufacturing reality, and operating performance. Working within Siemens' rigorous engineering process gave me deep appreciation for how world-class OEMs maintain design quality across complex rotating machinery.
